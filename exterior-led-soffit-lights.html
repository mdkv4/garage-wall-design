Installation Notes (QuinLED · Mean Well)

  1. CAUTION: Do not bend the strip. A bend can cause damage to the strip.
  2. CAUTION: Do not let metal touch the open contacts. Do not let the strip touch itself. It can cause a short circuit.
  3. At each of the six corners, cut the strip on the copper pads (3.125 cm marks). Connect a jumper across the cut for 24V, data and GND.
  4. Feeds: one feed is sufficient for 5 m. For 10 m at full output, feed the front and the end. For 20 m, use three feeds and a second data output.
  5. Dig-Quad power: seven output terminals and five ATO fuses. LED1 and LED2 use one fuse. LED3 and LED4 use one fuse. The board comes with 5 A fuses.
  6. The maximum current is 10 A continuous for each terminal and fuse, and 30 A continuous for the board.
  7. An end feed supplies approximately half the current of a middle feed. Select each fuse for its feed. Do not use a fuse above 10 A.
  8. Use the largest wire possible on the 9.5 mm input terminals (two wires, if possible). The output terminals are 5.08 mm.
  9. The four data outputs have level shifters (5.12 V). Each data output is correct for 5–10 m of cable from the controller.
  10. Above 60 W for each 5 m, install the strip in an aluminum cooling profile. If you do not, the life of the strip decreases.
  11. Use the W channel for white light. Do not use 100% RGB white. Do not operate the strip on its reel.
  12. On IP65 strip, seal each cut again. A cut breaks the factory seal.
  13. CAUTION: Do not set the PSU output above 24.0 V. The adjustment range goes to 30 V. A higher voltage can cause damage to the strip.
  14. PSU overload: the PSU limits the current at 105–125%. After 5 s, it stops.
  15. The PSU can supply 42 A. This is more than the 30 A rating of the Dig-Quad. Use a PSU-to-Dig-Quad cable that is rated for 30 A. Keep the WLED limit.
  16. Standby: the +12V-AUX output (0.5 A) is on when the 24 V output is off. Connect it to the Dig-Quad 5vEXT input. A relay on the Q1R trigger connects Remote ON/OFF to +12V-AUX. This turns on the 24 V output.
  17. The data input of the strip is not compatible with 3.3 V. The Dig-Quad outputs have level shifters. The strip warranty is 6 months.
  18. WARNING: Make sure that a qualified electrician does the AC connection to the PSU. AC voltage can kill you.

  1. Feed rule (QuinLED): the maximum distance between feeds is 10 m. Put one feed at each end. For 10 m, feed both ends. For 20 m, feed the start, the middle and the end.
  2. Use one fuse for each feed. LED1 and LED2 use the same fuse. LED3 and LED4 also use the same fuse. Use one terminal from each pair and the three other fused terminals.
  3. A middle feed supplies approximately two times the current of an end feed, because the current goes in two directions.
  4. Four data channels give the maximum frame rate. Each channel controls one 2-strip section (≈270 zones). The longest channel sets the frame time. In the QuinLED example, ≈640 zones on one output gives ≈30 FPS.
  5. Each channel starts at the feed nearest to the controller: CH1 at F2, CH2 and CH3 at F3, CH4 at F5. The CH1, CH2 and CH4 sections are reversed, so the data goes away from the start feed.
  6. CH1 to F2 is the longest data cable (≈9 m). The data range of the Dig-Quad is 5–10 m, so this cable is near the limit. There is no attic above the open corner of the L, so the cable cannot go in a straight line. Use the shortest attic route. If the cable length is near 10 m, install a data booster.
  7. Attic: the garage attic floor is on 2×4 sleepers (24" o.c., House Side to Street Side). There is a 1½" space above the truss chords for cables.
  8. The HEP-1000-24 is IP67. Its temperature range is −40 to +70 °C (refer to the derating curve). At ≈48% load, its margin is large. Install the Dig-Quad in an enclosure with vents.
  9. Cables F1, F2 and CH1 go from the garage attic into the house. Seal this hole in the garage-to-house separation with fire-stop material.
